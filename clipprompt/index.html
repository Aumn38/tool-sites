Derived prompt in Wan 3.0 format (copy it, use it, change it)

A warm, cinematic night-market scene: a street noodle vendor works his stall under neon signs as steam and hungry customers fill the frame. Shot 1 (0:00–0:03): Wide establishing shot of a bustling night-market alley at night, neon signs glowing red and yellow above a noodle stall, steam rising from a large wok as the vendor tosses noodles; camera slowly dollies in; sound of sizzling oil, crowd murmur and distant traffic. Shot 2 (0:03–0:07): Medium close-up of the vendor's hands pulling fresh noodles, flour dust drifting through warm tungsten light, rhythmic chopping on a wooden board behind him; camera holds steady with a slight handheld sway; sizzle and chopping sounds dominate. Shot 3 (0:07–0:10): Close-up of a steaming bowl handed across the counter, chopsticks lifting glossy noodles as steam curls toward the lens; low camera angle, shallow depth of field; a quick slurp and the vendor's friendly call. Shot 4 (0:10–0:12): Cut to a smiling customer at a small plastic stool, neon reflections shimmering on the wet pavement, the stall's warm glow behind him; camera slowly pulls back; laughter and night-market ambience. Continuity: same vendor in a dark apron, same red-and-yellow stall signage, same warm tungsten lighting and wet-pavement reflections throughout; no change of location or time of day.

Why the prompt is written this way

  • One-line description first, then numbered shots. The opener fixes subject, place and mood in one breath; the shots carry the sequence. That is the official Wan 3.0 pattern.
  • Time ranges are consecutive and never overlap (0:00–0:03, 0:03–0:07, 0:07–0:10, 0:10–0:12), and each shot carries one clear action. Treat a range as a time budget, not an exact edit point — never ask for three actions in one second.
  • Camera and sound are written into every shot, not left as decoration. “Slow dolly-in” and “sizzling oil” are part of the prompt, because Wan 3.0 generates native audio (dialogue, music, effects, ambience) by default.
  • The continuity line comes last. Identity, clothing, signage, lighting and layout are repeated here so the vendor, the stall and the wet pavement stay the same across cuts.
  • “One continuous take” is deliberately NOT used. That exact phrase forces no cuts; this clip has four cuts, so asking for one continuous take would fight the footage. Use it only for genuinely uncut clips.

How reverse-prompting works

  1. Watch. The live tool samples frames across the clip and reads the audio track, so motion and sound are observed, not guessed.
  2. Break down. Each shot gets subject, setting, motion, camera, style and sound — the same six slots the prompt formula uses.
  3. Compose. The slots are written up as one description, numbered shots with time ranges, and a continuity line, ready to paste into Wan 3.0.

What it cannot do — the honesty boundary

  • It recovers an equivalent prompt, not the exact original. Videos carry no prompt metadata, and generation is random — nobody can read back the precise words someone typed. What you get is a high-fidelity prompt that should reproduce a very similar clip.
  • Timestamps are intent signals, not frame-accurate edits. How strictly Wan 3.0 honours in-prompt timestamps has not been independently verified; treat ranges as “roughly this part of the clip”, not as cut points.
  • It cannot invent what is not in the footage. If a detail never appears on screen or on the soundtrack, a good reverse prompt leaves it out instead of hallucinating it.

FAQ

  • Does this tool generate videos? No. It writes prompts. You run the prompt yourself in Wan 3.0 (or any video model) — the preview does not generate anything.
  • Why is there no video upload? This is a preview build: the reverse engine is not connected yet, so there is nothing to upload to. Picking a file only previews it locally in your browser.
  • Will the reversed prompt reproduce my exact video? No — it reproduces a very similar one. The original wording is not recoverable from the video itself.
  • What clip lengths work? Wan 3.0 clips run 2–30 seconds at 30fps (480p / 720p / 1080p; 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:3, 3:4). The live tool will cap length and frame count per call to keep costs visible.
  • Is Wan 3.0 open-weights? No. Wan 2.2 is the last Apache flagship; Wan 3.0 weights are not open — do not pitch this as an open-model tool.
